**Retirement calculation review — 1 November 2026**

The daily ledger has useful account separation, explicit tax assumptions, shared market draws and a daily reconciliation. Nevertheless, there are material errors in CGT, fund-tax settlement, calendar mapping and the interpretation of retirement outcomes. A balanced ledger does not establish that the correct tax or economic event was recorded. Some findings increase projected wealth and others decrease it; their net effect on a personal plan has not been measured.

Priority notation: **P1** means correct before relying on affected retirement projections; **P2** means a narrower scenario error or material reporting limitation. These priorities are review judgements.

1. **P1 — Post-reform capital losses are applied in the wrong order.**

   Location: `src/financial_simulations/rules/au/tax.py:159`, particularly the pools at line 170.

   The implementation deliberately uses losses against post-reform gains before deferred gains. The amended s102-5 instead requires deferred non-residential gains to be reduced before ordinary non-residential gains; the taxpayer's choice operates within a category. This matters to the shares and funds actually modelled, without needing property support. [Enacted CGT amendments, Schedule 1, item 5](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00049/asmade/2026-06-26/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).

   Reproduction: $40,000 eligible deferred gain, $10,000 post-reform gain and $10,000 loss. Current net capital gain is $20,000 and the minimum-tax base is zero. Applying the required order gives `(40,000 - 10,000) / 2 + 10,000 = $25,000`, including a $10,000 minimum-tax base. The current result understates tax. Preserve pre-reform ordering for older events and implement the statutory categories from the reform date. Add mixed-category loss tests, including carried losses.

2. **P1 — The ledger omits the Age Pension exemption from minimum CGT tax.**

   Location: `src/financial_simulations/retirement/ledger.py:857`; the standalone input exists at `rules/au/tax.py:114`.

   The ledger supplies Age Pension taxable income and SAPTO eligibility but never sets `minimum_tax_exempt`. Section 119-15 exempts people who receive an Age Pension payment during the income year. [Enacted provision, s119-15](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00049/asmade/2026-06-26/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).

   Reproduction: the 2027-28 synthetic ledger receives $32,180.20 of Age Pension and nevertheless charges $1,349 of extra minimum CGT tax. That extra tax should be zero. Derive the exemption from actual qualifying receipts, not merely reaching pension age. Test a full pension, part pension, first qualifying payment and a means-tested zero payment.

3. **P1 — Non-refundable offsets are applied before adding minimum CGT tax.**

   Location: `src/financial_simulations/rules/au/tax.py:268` and line 278.

   The code first floors ordinary tax after offsets at zero, then adds minimum CGT tax. This discards offsets that could reduce that extra tax. The Treasury example explicitly allows offsets to reduce minimum tax. [Budget tax explainer, page 8](https://budget.gov.au/content/factsheets/download/tax-explainers-negative-gearing-capital-gains-tax.pdf).

   Reproduction: a 2027-28 resident with only a $10,000 post-reform gain is charged $3,000 and uses no LITO. Including the $700 LITO against total income tax gives $2,300, with no Medicare levy at that income. Compute the basic tax plus minimum-tax addition before applying relevant offsets and their refundability rules. Test LITO, SAPTO, WATO and franking separately. The methodology currently calls the existing order an engineering interpretation; it needs correction with the implementation.

4. **P1 — Accumulation tax disappears if the account was emptied or transferred to pension before 30 June.**

   Location: `src/financial_simulations/retirement/ledger.py:882`.

   Fund tax is calculated on the year's accumulation income, but collection is limited to the units remaining in accumulation. The unsatisfied amount is neither carried as a liability nor charged elsewhere. A negative tax/refund is also discarded if no accumulation units remain.

   Reproduction: $100,000 in accumulation, zero total returns, 4% assumed income yield, whole balance transferred to pension on 1 April. The annual record assesses $450 on the first three quarterly distributions; daily fund tax is $0 and no $450 debt is preserved. Accrue/reserve fund tax as income is earned, or carry a fund-level liability through transfers and withdrawals. Test transfers with positive and negative accrued tax, partial transfers and full withdrawals.

5. **P1 — Calendar weekdays accelerate trading returns.**

   Locations: `src/financial_simulations/retirement/analysis.py:284`, line 230, and `retirement/plan.py:351`.

   The future template includes every Monday-Friday and resamples one historical return for each. A normal trading history has fewer observations because of exchange holidays. The constant-CAGR reference explicitly uses a 252-observation conversion and then compounds on this denser grid.

   Reproduction: 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027 produces 260 return steps. A daily rate calibrated to 10% over 252 observations grows 10.3333% over these steps. Repeating that mismatch over 40 years produces approximately 12.9% extra terminal wealth before cash flows. This is an illustrative calculation, not an estimate for a personal plan. Both expected growth and volatility per calendar year are affected. Use a defined trading calendar or preserve empirical observations per calendar duration, alongside a separate calendar-day cash-flow ledger. Do not manufacture holiday return observations.

6. **P1 — Ending wealth and funded status ignore tax still payable.**

   Location: `src/financial_simulations/retirement/paths.py:86`.

   Outcomes use gross account values and only treat tax already overdue and unpaid as a failure. Assessed personal tax not yet due, pending Division 296 and the unassessed closing period are absent from headline ending wealth. This is distinct from optional tax on liquidation of unsold assets: some of these debts have already been incurred.

   Reproduction: all $120,000 of zero-cost-base holdings are sold to meet spending during 2026-27. At 30 June assets are $0, the assessed October tax bill is $9,620, but status is `funded` and unpaid tax is shown as $0. Retain a spending-through-horizon measure if useful, but also report net wealth after accrued liabilities and whether the plan can settle them. Reserve or settle closing liabilities for a solvency measure; show after-liquidation wealth separately if added.

7. **P2 — A weekend horizon can omit the entire final financial-year assessment.**

   Locations: `src/financial_simulations/retirement/analysis.py:284`, `retirement/plan.py:351`, `retirement/ledger.py:302` and line 855.

   The requested calendar endpoint is truncated to the final weekday, and `run_ledger` ends there. Reproduction: a plan from 1 July 2028 through 30 June 2029 actually ends on Friday 29 June. It records $4,000 of accumulation income but no annual assessment or fund tax; the intended $600 fund tax on that income is never charged. Separate the valuation-observation horizon from the accounting endpoint, carry the final valuation across the weekend and process 30 June without inventing a market return.

8. **P1 for an existing worker starting midyear — Missing opening tax-year state can create false refunds.**

   Location: `src/financial_simulations/retirement/ledger.py:374`, especially the zeroed year state at line 380.

   Mid-financial-year starts have no opening year-to-date salary, withholding, received contributions or investment-income inputs. All current-year totals start at zero and receive a full year's tax thresholds and caps. This is valid only if the missing part of the year truly had no relevant activity; that assumption is not captured in the plan.

   Reproduction: a continuing $104,000 salary is assessed at $23,480 over the full synthetic financial year. Starting the projection on 1 January records only $48,000 of remaining pay, assesses $5,265 and creates a $5,571.92 refund of the modelled withholding. Require opening YTD positions, or reject midyear starts for annual-tax projections until those inputs exist. Also account for opening pending employer contributions and existing liabilities.

9. **P2 — Multiple non-concessional payments double-count bring-forward usage.**

   Locations: `src/financial_simulations/retirement/ledger.py:765` and `rules/au/superannuation.py:167`.

   The ledger passes cumulative current-year contributions to a function that adds its argument to the open period's already-used amount. Reproduction: an eligible person contributes $200,000 then $50,000 against a $390,000 three-year cap. The second call compares $250,000 with the remaining $190,000 and incorrectly rejects the payment, despite actual total contributions being only $250,000. Use an incremental contribution API or recompute from a fixed opening-year bring-forward state. Test three payments in the trigger year and repeated payments in subsequent years. The current planner does not expose this contribution schedule; the public ledger API does.

10. **P2 — Voluntary contributions after age 75 are accepted without the receipt-date restriction.**

    Locations: `src/financial_simulations/rules/au/superannuation.py:151` and `retirement/ledger.py:765`.

    Being over 75 only removes bring-forward eligibility in the helper; it still returns an ordinary annual cap, and the ledger never checks whether the fund may accept the payment. A synthetic 76-year-old successfully contributes $50,000 as an ordinary NCC. The ordinary voluntary contribution window ends 28 days after the month in which the member turns 75; compulsory employer and downsizer contributions have distinct rules. [ATO contribution acceptance guidance](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-6c3220ce-b973-4514-84e0-0f9f28c4d0a7).

    Check contribution type and the actual receipt date independently of cap arithmetic, including salary sacrifice. This primarily affects direct ledger NCC scenarios and working-past-75 scenarios.

11. **P2 — Working pensioners' assessable earnings and opening Work Bonus are wrong.**

    Locations: `src/financial_simulations/retirement/ledger.py:723`, line 353 and `rules/au/age_pension.py:86`.

    The ledger sends `gross - sacrifice` into the Age Pension income test. Services Australia includes salary-sacrifice amounts in employment income. Once Work Bonus is exhausted, a $500 sacrifice can incorrectly reduce assessed earnings by $500 and raise an income-tested pension by up to $250 a fortnight. [Services Australia employment income](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/employment-income?context=22526).

    Independently, the Work Bonus bank starts at zero rather than applying the $4,000 commencement credit for a newly eligible recipient. [DSS Work Bonus examples](https://guides.dss.gov.au/social-security-guide/3/1/15/40). These errors act in opposite directions and do not reliably cancel. Use gross earnings; represent commencement/claim state and an opening bank balance, with the legislated initial credit when applicable. The probe confirms $1,000 gross, $500 sacrifice and $300 Work Bonus become only $200 plus deeming in the current income test.

12. **P2 — Truncating a path invents a quarter-end distribution and shifts pension instalments.**

    Location: `src/financial_simulations/retirement/ledger.py:327`.

    `_last_observation_per` treats the final observation of a truncated month or quarter as its period end. Reproduction: zero-return $100,000 holdings with 4% income yield pay $1,000 on 15 July if the path ends then, but pay $0 through 15 July if the same path extends through September. Identical prefixes should produce identical account histories. Build scheduled period-end events independently of the chosen horizon and specify partial-period conventions. The same issue affects the `month_ends` set used for pension minimum instalments.

13. **P2 — Super distributions and tax depend on the order of asset names.**

    Location: `src/financial_simulations/retirement/ledger.py:686` through the reinvestment at line 711.

    One asset's distribution is immediately reinvested across all target weights before the next asset's distribution is calculated. Later assets therefore distribute on units just bought with an earlier distribution, and some purchases occur before that later asset's ex-distribution price adjustment. Reversing otherwise identical asset columns changes financial outcomes.

    Reproduction: zero total returns, $80,000 A/$20,000 B, target weights 20%/80%, yields 4%/8%. A-then-B incurs $732.58 fund tax; B-then-A incurs $725.51. The balances differ by $7.07 after just one year. Calculate all entitlements from one holdings snapshot, apply all ex-distribution price changes, then reinvest. Add an asset-permutation invariance check.

14. **P2 — Reported withdrawal rates mix incompatible inflation bases and omit Division 296.**

    Locations: `src/financial_simulations/retirement/real.py:34`, line 40, and `retirement/paths.py:53`.

    Flows are deflated in whole financial-year steps, but opening wealth is deflated by exact elapsed days. Dividing the two does not preserve the nominal withdrawal ratio, contrary to the app's explanation. Example: a 1 November opening, 10% CPI stress input and a nominal 4% withdrawal over the following financial year become about 3.8724% using the current deflators. The discrepancy is smaller at the default CPI but systematic for off-July starts. Calculate a nominal rate directly or convert numerator and denominator to the same valuation date. Date-deflate real cash flows if describing actual purchasing power, rather than making the series flat by construction.

    The numerator at `paths.py:56` also excludes the separate `division_296` flow, even though its description says spending plus tax less Age Pension. Decide which taxes the measure includes and use that same definition in code and captions.

15. **P2 — Withdrawal-strategy claims overstate what the calculations prove.**

    Locations: `src/financial_simulations/retirement/analysis.py:149`, `ui/app.py:1219`, line 1235 and line 439.

    `perpetual_withdrawal_rates` only requires starting real capital to remain at the end of a finite sampled window. It does not calculate the probability of sustaining withdrawals indefinitely. Rename it to a rate preserving real capital at the planning age; extend the stochastic horizon and state its duration if longer-run survival is desired. The 30-year 4% rule is not the same criterion as preserving all starting real capital.

    The UI also says bootstrap resampling cannot generate a sequence worse than the worst historical stretch. Blocks can repeat and concatenate bad periods, producing longer and worse cumulative losses than the original history. Individual sampled daily observations remain bounded by the dataset, which is a different limitation.

    Finally, an annually reset percentage-of-wealth rule can run out between reviews, encounter inaccessible super or be forced above its affordable level by an essential floor. Replace its unconditional cannot-run-out claim with the actual annual-review behaviour.

**Documented approximations and improvement suggestions**

- Prioritise a joint AUD asset/FX treatment. The planner explicitly relabels sampled USD returns as an AUD proxy; this is disclosed but leaves Australian spending exposed to unmodelled currency risk. Keep nominal AUD tax bases, daily returns and separate calendar events.
- The 20% super gain-realisation share, no fund capital losses, no embedded opening gains, universal one-third discount on realised fund gains, untaxed super switches and assumed distribution yields are material approximations. They do not constitute instrument-level tax accounting. Stress them independently and distinguish uncertainty about fund behaviour from tax-rule errors.
- Retirement success should distinguish bridge funding, essential-spending adequacy, desired-spending adequacy, policy-induced cuts and ability to settle taxes. The current funded status can look reassuring when a rule dramatically cuts living standards. Existing spending-cut outputs help, but add explicit adequacy probabilities and duration/severity measures.
- Use more retirement paths for tail claims and report uncertainty intervals. With 100 independent paths and zero observed failures, the one-sided 95% binomial upper bound is approximately `1 - 0.05**(1/100) = 2.95%`, even before uncertainty about the market model. Compare seeds, block lengths, historical windows and alternative generators.
- Preserve the disclosed conditioning on supported paths, but make its consequences prominent. Excluding paths that hit contribution caps or other unsupported rules can change both upper and lower tails. Prefer representing supported excess-contribution resolution policies when those scenarios matter.
- Refresh the current methodology status. `PLAN.md` still contains statements that the ledger stops before Age Pension age; parts of the ledger methodology say no realised fund gains; ADR 0023 justifies omitting the last-15-years Guyton-Klinger rule using the old short horizon. Preserve historical decisions but clearly supersede those assumptions and label the implemented spending rule as a variant where it differs.
- The review's synthetic ledger PNG formats sub-$1,000 monthly flows with repeated `0k`/`-0k` labels. Use dollar labels or one decimal place at that scale. No layout defect was apparent in the inspected retirement-range PNG. Interactive rendering remains unverified in this review.

**Suggested correction sequence**

First fix the three CGT errors, fund-tax liabilities, and terminal liabilities. Then correct the trading/calendar endpoint treatment and opening YTD state, followed by contribution and working-pensioner boundaries. Add the reproductions as regression tests with expectations checked against the cited legal rules or simple arithmetic. Re-run paired retirement paths using the same source data, seed and plan, reporting changes in tax, net wealth, bridge failures and spending adequacy. The review does not establish the sign or size of the combined change for a private scenario.
